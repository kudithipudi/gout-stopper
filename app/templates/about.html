What is gout?

Gout is a form of arthritis caused by too much uric acid in the blood. Uric acid forms when your body breaks down purines — natural compounds found in many foods and drinks. When levels get high, sharp urate crystals can form in a joint, most often the big toe, causing sudden, intense pain, swelling, and redness.

Some people have a genetic tendency; others are affected by diet, alcohol, dehydration, some medications, or kidney function. An attack can last a few days to a couple of weeks.

How diet factors in

Diet is only part of the picture, but it's a part you control. Foods very high in purines raise uric acid the most. The general guidance is:

Avoid — very high purine

  • Organ meats: liver, kidney, sweetbreads
  • Red meat and game meat in large amounts
  • Sardines, anchovies, mackerel, herring
  • Shellfish: mussels, scallops, shrimp, lobster
  • Beer (especially) and other alcohol
  • Sugar-sweetened drinks and high-fructose foods

Limit — moderate purine

  • Poultry and some fish (salmon, tuna, trout)
  • Dried beans, lentils, chickpeas, soy
  • Oatmeal, asparagus, cauliflower, mushrooms
  • Keep portions reasonable rather than cutting them out

Good choices

  • Low-fat dairy — milk, yogurt, cheese (may lower uric acid)
  • Eggs, nuts, whole grains, rice, pasta, bread
  • Most fruits and vegetables, especially cherries and berries
  • Lots of water — hydration helps flush uric acid
  • Coffee and tea in moderation

Lifestyle tips

  • Drink plenty of water every day.
  • Limit alcohol, especially beer.
  • Maintain a healthy weight; avoid crash dieting, which can trigger attacks.
  • Limit sugar-sweetened drinks.
  • If prescribed urate-lowering medication, take it consistently.
